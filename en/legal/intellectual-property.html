2. Distinction Between Client Trademarks and Apparel Design Authorship

In custom garment manufacturing, team uniforms, and digital embroidery digitizing, Kingdom strictly distinguishes two legal domains under Venezuelan law:

  • Distinctive Signs Supplied by Clients (Industrial Property Law): The client retains all ownership and usage rights over pre-existing wordmarks, brand logos, and emblems provided for garment manufacturing or embroidery.
  • Authorship of Textile Graphic Design and Composition (Copyright Law / Ley sobre el Derecho de Autor): Pursuant to the Venezuelan Copyright Law (Ley sobre el Derecho de Autor, 1993), all works of the intellect of a creative nature—including illustration, color composition, vector layout, and aesthetic garment adaptation—constitute protected works whose original author is Ramses Martínez (Kingdom) by the sole fact of creation.
  • Absence of Implied Assignment of Economic Rights: Under the Venezuelan Copyright Law (1993), any assignment or transfer of economic exploitation rights must be interpreted restrictively and must be expressly documented in writing. Payment solely for garment production or printing does not transfer intellectual property or copyright assignment over the garment design unless the client has contracted and formalized a separate written copyright assignment for the custom textile artwork.
  • User License for DST/PES Embroidery Files: Digital embroidery files and stitch punching delivered to clients convey a license of use for the physical production of agreed garments, while Ramses Martínez retains technical authorship over the master stitch files.
  • Authorship & Portfolio Exhibition: Ramses Martínez retains recognition of authorship over his creations. Kingdom may show completed work as a reference, together with the ownership notices on this site.
