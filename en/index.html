Frequently Asked Questions

Technical FAQ

Key details regarding embroidery punching, fabric compatibility, delivery formats, and payments.

What do I need to send to request an embroidery matrix?

A clear image or vector file of your logo or artwork (JPG, PNG, PDF, AI, or EPS), specifying desired dimensions in centimeters and the target fabric or garment type (piqué, twill, drill, cap, etc.) to calibrate stitch density and pull compensation.

Which formats are the embroidery matrices delivered in?

They are delivered primarily in DST format (industry standard compatible with Tajima, Barudan, SWF, and most machines) and PES (standard for Brother and Babylock). If your machine requires a specific format (such as EXP or JEF), let us know during your inquiry.

How are digital files delivered?

Digital files for DST/PES matrices or vector artwork are delivered directly via email or WhatsApp, ready to load onto your embroidery machine or send to production.

How are international payments and local orders handled?

For international clients (digital digitizing and vector artwork), quotation and payment coordination are conducted via email or WhatsApp. For local Venezuelan orders (physical garments and manufacturing), payment and shipping details are arranged directly via WhatsApp.

What is the difference between Sublimation, DTF, and Embroidery?

Sublimation chemically dyes polyester fibers (Dry-Fit) with zero tactile weight and full breathability, ideal for full-print athletic uniforms. DTF heat-transfers a digital film onto cotton and blends in any color, perfect for streetwear tees. Embroidery stitches high-durability polyester threads with rich texture, optimal for corporate shirts, jackets, and caps with institutional prestige.

How are design or matrix revisions handled?

Before final delivery or production, a visual simulation or stitch preview is verified with the client to confirm dimensions, typography, spelling, and color distribution.

Can I request an embroidery sample or stitch test?

For corporate uniforms or bulk apparel orders, a sample stitchout on a fabric swatch of the actual garment is always recommended to adjust thread tension before running the full batch.
